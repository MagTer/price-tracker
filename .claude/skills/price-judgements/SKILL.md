# Price judgements

The judgements behind every number on every surface — portal, buy-list mail and MCP. How the portal draws them is the `portal-ui` skill.

Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` at `88e99e9` by the information-layout move (`git log -- .claude/skills/price-judgements/SKILL.md` names the commit). A pointer of the form "→ X bullet" names a bullet by its subject; it may sit in another project skill — `AGENTS.md` lists all four.

## kr/enhet and the 04.1 shape

**The 04.1 shape — a product is abstract, a link is concrete:**

- **`package_size` + `package_quantity` live on `ProductStore`** (the link), not on `Product`. `unit` (st/liter/kg) stays on `Product`. The product is the abstract thing you compare; the link is the specific package a specific store sells.

- **`PricePoint.unit_price_sek` is GONE.** kr/unit is **computed on read** from `price / link.package_quantity`, exposed as the hybrid `PricePoint.computed_unit_price_sek` (Python + SQL modes). **The single definition lives in `src/domain/pricing.py`. Do not write a second one anywhere.** Correcting a link's amount retroactively fixes all its history — that's the point.

- **`PricePoint.store_unit_price_sek`** is the store's *printed* comparison price. It is displayed beside the computed value and **never sorted on** — stores print in different units, so sorting on it compares kr/kg against kr/st. **Since v0.58.1 it is never displayed without its measure either**, and that is the same rule one step further: the links panel rendered it as a bare number, so a `st` product at ICA showed "Jfr-pris (kr/st) 6,81 kr · Butiken anger 63,20 kr" — two measures in one row with nothing saying which is which, and a 9x gap that reads as a data error. `storeSaysKr` in admin.html labels it from the wire's `store_unit_price_unit` (`pricing.printed_measure`, carried by BOTH Gotcha-4 twins) and prints bare kronor when no extractor recorded a measure. **`unitKr()` must never be used for this cell** — it labels with the PRODUCT's unit, so it would print ICA's kr/kg as "63,20 kr/st", a wrong measure stated with confidence and worse than the silence it replaced. Gated in `test_static_gates.py`.

- **`ProductStore.scraped_package_quantity`** is what the page said, kept as *evidence*. Typed input is *intent*. Evidence autofills an empty field and **flags** a conflict; it never overwrites intent.

## Deals: verdict and timing

- **A deal is judged on TWO axes since v0.48.0, and both live in `domain/deals.py`.** The verdict is space (cheapest of the product's links right now); **`timing` is time** — this price against `lowest_unit_price_sek`, the product's cheapest kr/enhet OBSERVED across all its links inside `PRICE_LOW_WINDOW_DAYS` (84). `poor` = at least `SEEN_CHEAPER_MIN_PCT` (10 %) above that floor, `good` = under it, `unknown` = no comparable history. Born in prod 2026-08-04: Bryggkaffe Mellanrost 450 g at ICA Björksätra, "2 för 130 kr" = 144,44 kr/kg, cheapest of three links and therefore BEST — while the same coffee had been 110,00 kr/kg at Willys nine days earlier. It led the buy list; five of the six rows beside it sat at **0,0 %** above their own floor, which is what a genuine campaign looks like (it sets the new low). **Ten percent, and not the span bar's position:** the bar's position moves with an outlier HIGH, which is not evidence about this price, and a tighter threshold would demote real campaigns on quantity-rounding noise alone. **The floor query counts INACTIVE links** — the deliberate opposite of the best-alt rule — because "have I seen this cheaper" is about a price that was really charged, not about a shelf we can reach today. A `poor` row is **demoted, never hidden**: Att köpa puts it in its own collapsed section above the WORSE one, with the percentage, the floor price, the butik and the date in the row; the buy-list email drops it exactly as it drops WORSE (the email IS the buy list, and the two must not disagree — Gotcha 4); MCP carries the fields. Consequently the page heading counts what SURVIVES both filters and says "värda att köpa nu", not "billigast just nu" — the old wording would undercount things that genuinely are billigast just nu, and claimed nothing about whether buying them was a good idea.

## Prices a human observed, and the offline paths

**A price a human observed is an ordinary price point, and that is the whole design (v0.49.0).** Some offers never touch the page the tracker fetches: ICA Björksätra advertises förbokning campaigns on Facebook, and that page is behind a login and a bot sensor exactly like the Willys blad listing — **discovery stays human, by the same decision** (→ v0.46.0). But the OBSERVATION is not a second kind of truth: same link, same package, a known day, a price that really could be paid. So `POST /product-stores/{id}/prices` writes a `PricePoint` with `raw_data = {"source": "manual"}` and nothing else is invented — **and note the contrast with a blad offer, which is store-scoped and belongs to no link, and therefore is still never persisted.** Three deliberate omissions: **`last_checked_at` is untouched** (we did not look at the store's page — the v0.29.2 rule), **no `check_attempts` row** (that table measures what the STORES answered; a keystroke is not a request and would corrupt the one dataset built to judge them), and **no `store_unit_price_sek`** (no printed jämförpris exists, so the validator skips the row instead of comparing our arithmetic with itself). An ordinarie makes it an offer in the v0.25.2 shape with `offer_type="manuellt pris"`, so the deal row, the email and the history table all show that a human typed it; without one it is simply a price that was charged. The date is a Swedish civil day converted at **midday**, not midnight — a note dated today must land AFTER that morning's scheduled check to become the link's latest point, which is what puts it in this week's buy list; backdating it makes it history and floor only. `offer >= price` is refused here too (v0.32.1), because this is the one place a human can type the inversion directly.

- **The two offline paths are one card since v0.52.0, and the split between them is the design.** Both exist because some prices never touch the page the tracker fetches, and both keep discovery human. What differs is what happens after: a **blad offer is a LOOKUP** (store-scoped, week-scoped, "olika sorter", belongs to no link) and is still never persisted, while a **price the reader decides IS their tracked product** is an ordinary observation and becomes a price point. The bridge between them (`notable_links` → the "Notera på …" button) fills the form and stops — it must never write on its own, because attributing an offer to a product is exactly the judgement the LLM ladder is not trusted with either. And it must never offer a link at another store: `notable_links` is filtered on the offer's own `store_slug` for the same reason the manual endpoint records `source: "manual"` — an observation has to keep saying where it came from.

## Pristillfällen

**Pristillfällen is the only judgement in this app about the PAST (v0.55.0).** Everything else asks about now — `deals.verdict` compares a product's links at this moment, `deals.timing` compares a price with the product's own floor. This asks whether the campaign we already showed was actually the cheapest way to buy the product THAT WEEK, which makes it the only feedback the tracker gives on its own advice, and the only place a store's own discount percentage gets an answer ("−22 %, and Willys was still 8 % cheaper per kg"). `domain/stats.product_offer_occasions` is THE walk and **`stats.was_cheapest` is THE comparison**, shared with the per-store "varav faktiskt billigast" counter — a `<` in one and a `<=` in the other would make a number disagree with the rows behind it. Four rules it must keep:

- **The verdict is taken BEFORE the point is folded into the series**, so "elsewhere" means the alternatives as they stood, carried forward from each link's last observation. Points from before the window seed that basis and are not themselves rows: a campaign that ran before the period is not in the period.

- **`was_cheapest` is None, never False, when no other link had a known price.** Calling an unjudgeable campaign "not cheapest" invents a loss out of a gap. The row still appears — dropping it would stop the rows from matching the count above them.

- **INACTIVE links are included, unlike everywhere else in `stats.py`** (decided 2026-08-13). That filter exists so "billigast" cannot name a shelf you may not buy from; this table is history, and the same reasoning makes `deals.observed_spans` count inactive links for the floor. A retired link is flagged in the row, because a row the reader cannot act on should say so.

- **The alternative carries `alternative_seen_at`.** With weekly checks the price a campaign is judged against can be six days old; a row saying "Willys låg 8 % under" without saying when it was last seen claims a precision we do not have — the same honesty as the buy list's "sett fredag 24/7".

Its own endpoint rather than a field on `/stats`: that payload carries one row per product for a page showing all of them, and every product's campaign history hung off it would grow the whole table for a view that reads one product at a time. It takes **`days`**, not weeks, because the modal draws the chart and this table from ONE window (`HISTORY_DAYS`) and a window that differed by a day would put a campaign in one and not the other.

## Prisutveckling (statistics)

**Prisutveckling (Statistik until v0.47.0) answers cross-product questions, and `domain/stats.py` is THE definition (v0.36.0).** The page is the bigger sibling of the per-product history modal, and since v0.58.0 the product name in "Per produkt" is the way INTO it: this table is the one place that states a product moved N % over a period, so "moved how, and when" is the question it hands the reader, and it used to lead nowhere. Four rules make its numbers true, and each of them fails SILENTLY and plausibly if broken:

- **kr/unit is never summed or averaged across products** — milk's kr/l plus cheese's kr/kg is nonsense, the same invariant that keeps `store_unit_price_sek` unsortable. Everything cross-product is a PERCENTAGE against that product's own baseline. That is also why v1 has **no basket index**: an index needs chain-linking to survive a product being added (it is added weekly here), and the per-product table answers the same question without the machinery. Weights ("how much do I buy per month") stay unbuilt and optional — an unweighted view needs no input at all.

- **Store comparison uses a MATCHED basket** — only products at least two stores carry. Over full assortments the number compares what each store happens to SELL, not what it charges. A store with several links for one product competes with its own cheapest pack.

- **Carry forward, never interpolate.** A link checked last Monday holds Monday's price all week — that is what the shelf says. A price observed BEFORE the window is the baseline the period opens at, so narrowing the period cannot empty the table.

- **Absence is not zero.** A product observed once has `change_pct = None` and renders as an em dash: "0,0 %" would be a confident claim about a period we did not watch. Every panel prints what it was computed from, which is why the page needs no "come back in six weeks" gate — it matures on its own.

**The statistics page has no write path, and that is a rule, not an omission (decided 2026-07-28).** Not an auto-tuner, not an "apply this schedule" button. Raising check frequency where prices move most means fetching hardest exactly where the WAF lives, and a wall records no price point — so the product that changes most often would come to look like it had stopped. The loop would eat the measurement it is built on. Weekday and volatility patterns are shown as observations; any schedule change is made by hand in the link dialog. The coverage counts link INTO the Produkter facets rather than repeating the lists there.

## The validator

**The validator asks whether the tracker still SEES what the stores publish, and `domain/validation.py` is THE judgement (v0.44.0).** Born from the jättefranska miss: every ICA check said "ok" while every ICA campaign was invisible — no error anywhere, plausible prices, wrong answer. Two layers, both built on redundancy already recorded, both read-only like statistics (observe, never act, never touch scheduling):

- **Tier regression** — `EXPECTED_SOURCE` maps each store with a structured best tier (ica→ica_page, rusta→rusta_page, lyko→lyko_page, jysk→jysk_page, willys→willys_api) and flags any active link whose LATEST `outcome="ok"` attempt answered from a lower tier: the structured source changed shape and the ladder silently degraded. Judged on ok-attempts only (failures belong to link_health, walls to the breaker; one healthy check clears by construction). **Clas Ohlson is deliberately absent** — its extractor returns None BY DESIGN when the page adds nothing beyond JSON-LD, so "jsonld" is normal there and judging it would be a standing false alarm.

- **Jämförpris contradiction** — the store's printed unit price against `price/package_quantity` on each link's latest point: a disagreement beyond tolerance (1.5 % relative AND 0.10 kr absolute — quantity rounding gives 0.4 % noise, the one real prod error sat at 20 %) means the price or the amount is wrong; it catches both failure classes with stored data. **A link passes when EITHER basis matches** — Willys/ICA print the jämförpris off ORDINARIE but Clas Ohlson prints it off the CAMPAIGN price (both verified on prod 2026-08-03), and modeling which store does which is a maintenance treadmill refusing-either avoids. **The comparison is vetoed outright when the store PRINTS in another measure than the product's unit** — ICA prints toalettpapper in kr/KG while the product is counted in rulle (63.20 vs 6.81, both correct, ~89 % "deviation": the false alarms the first Fel & luckor survey was full of). That reading lives in **`pricing.printed_measure`** (v0.58.1 — it was private here), off the code each extractor stashes in `raw_data` (ICA `unit_price_unit`, Willys/Rusta `comparison_unit`). It is shared with the links panel, which LABELS the same figure with it, and the sharing is the point: a second marker table would let a row rendered "63,20 kr/kg" be judged as kr/st. **None means UNKNOWN and the two callers must diverge on it** — validation keeps judging (going blind on every store that records no code is worse than a rare false alarm), the UI stays silent (a label naming the wrong measure is worse than no label).

- **Surfaces:** `/stats` composes `data_quality` into its payload (the endpoint composes two domain definitions; validation ignores the period filter — quality is about NOW); **since v0.47.0 the findings own a page — Fel & luckor** (→ faults-page bullet), where they sit above the gaps as the tracker's silent failures; the buy-list email carries the issues as a ride-along block, **only when non-empty**, and a validator crash mails the buy list without it rather than nothing (telemetry must not kill what it rides in — the check_attempts rule again).

## The buy-list email

**The buy-list email goes out on the days something is CHECKED, and its recipient is configuration (v0.41.0, days derived v0.59.0).** `_check_summary_due` sends once per local check day from the END of that day's förmiddag window — 12:00 **Swedish wall-clock** via `schedule.summary_slot`, the first moment the morning's checks are in — and **which days those are comes from `schedule.summary_weekdays`, the union of every ACTIVE link's effective weekdays**, cached once per local day in the scheduler and falling back to Monday if the read fails or nothing runs on a weekday at all (degrade to the fallback, never to silence: an empty day set looks exactly like "no day qualifies" and would stop the mail with nothing saying so). **It was hardcoded to Monday until v0.59.0 while the stores checked on cadences of their own, and that gap is what shipped a dead campaign**: a Rusta rea observed on Saturday 22/8 reached the 24/8 mail after it had ended. It was not bad luck — 72 h and 7 days are coprime, so an interval link's check walks around the week and lands over 48 h out roughly a third of the time (measured 2026-08-27 over five prod Mondays: 20 of 56 link-Mondays). The fix is BOTH halves — mån+fre everywhere (→ schedule bullet) so the observation is hours old, and the mail's days read off that schedule so a future edit to it cannot leave the mail behind. Consequently `DEAL_STALE_HOURS` (48) means something sharper now: not "old", but **"this row's check did not land"** — a failure or a wall, since the longest normal gap is Friday→Monday. **The dedup is a ROW, not a memory (v0.62.0), and only a send that returned True writes it (v0.41.2)** — `ResendEmailService.send` never raises, so failure is a RETURN VALUE, and the original code discarded it: a Resend outage at noon lost the day's buy list while `summaries_sent` and the log claimed success. A False (or an exception) writes no receipt, so the 5-minute loop retries for the rest of the local day. **It was in-memory until v0.62.0, with the duplicate written off as acceptable** ("a rare duplicate after an afternoon restart is the accepted cost") — and that premise expired without anyone noticing: v0.59.0 doubled the mail to Monday AND Friday, and a deploy IS a restart, so the duplicate started arriving exactly when a release did. Measured on prod 2026-09-04: the v0.61.0 container mailed the buy list **6 seconds after startup**, hours after that morning's send. `summary_sends.sent_on` is the local check day and the PRIMARY KEY, so a duplicate is now impossible by construction rather than by remembering; `_last_summary_date` survives only as a per-cycle cache in front of it. **The read fails toward the DUPLICATE on purpose** — a database hiccup at noon answers "not sent" and mails again, because a second copy is an annoyance while a missing buy list is the product silently not working; that is the opposite of how an auth guard degrades, and the difference is that this is a nuisance-versus-silence decision, not a security one. The row also answers a question nothing else could: **did the mail go out, and what did it carry** (recipient, deal count, watched count, whether the data-quality block rode along) — no row means not sent, the same rule `check_attempts` follows. Note the one duplicate the fix cannot prevent: the release that INTRODUCES the table starts with it empty, so a deploy landing on a check day after 12:00 sends once more. (The old rule was "Monday 14:00" applied on naive UTC — 15/16 Swedish depending on DST, the same silent drift v0.33.0 removed from the check window.) Content is the portal's "Värt att köpa" as an email: **BEST + UNKNOWN from `domain/deals.py`, WORSE filtered out in the scheduler** — an offer another link beats per unit is information, not news — grouped per butik by the notifier (one section per butik = one leg of the shopping round); the subject NAMES the day (`Inköpslista måndag – Prisspaning`) because two mails a week sharing one subject read as a duplicate, and any row older than `DEAL_STALE_HOURS` is dated ("sett fredag 24/7 — kan ha hunnit ta slut"), never hidden. **Since v0.53.2 the third column is the SPAN BAR, not the margin against another butik** — "0,45 kr/kg billigare än ICA Maxi 1 kg" answered the wrong question for a buy list: cheaper than the next link says nothing about whether *either* price is any good, and both can sit at the top of a span the product has spent twelve weeks below while the row still reads as a win. The margin survives as the SORT KEY inside each butik's section. The bar is **tables and percentage widths, never absolute positioning** (Outlook renders through Word, which has no `position`, and a collapsed bar would take the row's only judgement with it) and the fill's right edge IS the marker — an email cannot afford a second element overlapping the first to say the same thing. It makes the portal's three refusals in the portal's words (no jfr-pris / one observation / the price has not moved), and a row whose link has no mängd says that ONCE: the span cell already names the fix, so the UNKNOWN verdict line stays quiet. `_span_html` in `notifier.py` and `dealSpanHtml` in `admin.html` are twin RENDERINGS of one dataset (the deal row's own lowest/highest — there is no second query); email HTML cannot share the portal's stylesheet, so keep their wording and thresholds in step by hand. No top-N cap — the old top-10-by-recency silently dropped real wins. **The recipient is `SUMMARY_EMAIL`, falling back to `ALLOWED_ENTRA_EMAIL`** (the Entra UPN — set `SUMMARY_EMAIL` when that is not a deliverable address), and is NEVER derived from watch rows: the old sender mailed the union of watch emails, so a tracker with products but no alarm sent nothing — the digest existed only as a side effect of the watch feature. The watched-products section (lowest current kr/enhet per bevakad produkt) still rides along. Per-product watch alerts are unchanged and remain the "tell me NOW" channel.

## MCP

**MCP surface: four tools, and the module docstrings in `src/mcp_server/server.py` are THE description.** `check_price` (last OBSERVED price per listing — reads history, never fetches), `compare_stores` (every tracked package of one product, ranked on the COMPUTED kr/enhet, amount-less listings last and saying so), `find_deals` (every current offer, no cap, each row carrying `verdict` AND `timing` plus villkor and lager — → deals bullet), `list_products`. **Do not read the surface off EXTRACTION.md §5**: that table is the May-2026 spec and is superseded on both halves a caller cares about — the mount was still an open question there ("`/mcp` path OR a separate Traefik subdomain. Decide during Phase 4" — decided by D-29) and its "List of current offers" predates every judgement `find_deals` now renders. A tool's output is user-facing text, so it is Swedish and it follows the portal's wording: change one and check the other two surfaces (portal, buy-list email) for the Gotcha-4 drift.
