# Store fetching

Getting a request through to a store, and recording what the store refused. How a page is read once it arrives is the `store-extractors` skill.

Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` at `88e99e9` by the information-layout move (`git log -- .claude/skills/store-fetching/SKILL.md` names the commit). A pointer of the form "→ X bullet" names a bullet by its subject; it may sit in another project skill — `AGENTS.md` lists all four.

## Schedule

- **The check schedule is a STORE property (v0.13.0), and since v0.59.0 EVERY store runs the same two days:** `Store.check_weekdays` (JSONB list, 0=måndag — **`[0, 4]` for all eleven stores**, alembic 0013; it was ICA `[0]`, Willys `[0, 4]` and interval mode for the other nine) + `Store.check_frequency_hours` (72, now only reached by a link that overrides itself into interval mode), both modes morning-aligned (06–12 — **Europe/Stockholm wall-clock since v0.33.0**: the window and the weekday are computed in `STORE_TIMEZONE` and converted back to the app-wide naive-UTC storage convention, because "förmiddag" and "måndag" are Swedish civil concepts; computed directly on UTC they silently meant 08–14 Swedish summer time, drifted an hour at each DST change, and would judge a check at 00:30 Swedish Monday night as Sunday's. **Instants stay naive UTC everywhere — only the civil-rule computation and the UI's `parseUtc` display layer are timezone-aware; never store local time**). A link's own `check_weekdays`/`check_frequency_hours` are NULL in the normal state = inherit; setting either overrides WHOLESALE. Resolution + next-check time live in `domain/schedule.py` — one definition, used by the scheduler and the admin `/frequency` endpoint alike (its private copy was removed; do not reintroduce one); **since v0.39.0 both resolve through `next_check_time_for_link`, which STRATIFIES the förmiddag slot**: `weekday_slot` (also in `schedule.py` — THE sibling lookup, never copy its query) ranks the link among the store's active links landing on the same weekday and places link *i* of *n* at `start + (i + 0.5) × window/n` ±25 % of a stratum, at second resolution. The old free draw (independent random hour/minute, `second=0` — 360 whole-minute slots) was uniform in expectation but lumpy in practice: prod had 4 pairs of ICA links on the exact same minute (expected — birthday math), and same-minute due times drain through the rate limiter back to back, the burst pattern that walls ICA. Interval mode and retries keep the free draw — no sibling set to stratify over. Quick-add and the add-link dialog ask nothing about scheduling; only the link-edit dialog offers the override. **There is no store-level schedule endpoint — a store's days change by migration only**, which is what alembic 0013 is; a per-link override is the in-app answer. **And the buy-list email's days are READ off these columns** (`schedule.summary_weekdays`, → buy-list-email bullet): mail on the days something is checked, so the two cannot drift apart. They had drifted — the mail assumed a Monday cadence only ICA and Willys followed.

- **ICA's Monday prices are live shortly after MIDNIGHT, not in the morning — measured, unharvested until 2026-08-14, and still an OPEN lever.** A one-off cron probe ran across the night of 2–3 August 2026 — it lived outside this repo until 2026-09-06 and now sits in `docs/experiments/ica-night-probe/` (`probe.py`, `results.jsonl`, `RESULTAT.md`, `README.md`, `cron.log`), because both pointers to it were absolute paths into a dev VM that `make rebuild-dev` can now replace in one command to answer one binary question — does ICA flip prices in a midnight batch or by hand in the morning? Four products at two ICA butiker, six runs. **Bregott Maxi went 39,90 → 48,95 somewhere between Sun 22:30 and Mon 00:15 Swedish**, and nothing moved after that through 06:00. So Monday's shelf is already true by ~00:15, while `check_weekdays = [0]` + the 06–12 window means a link can be checked as late as noon: the buy list a reader opens at breakfast may still hold Sunday's prices, and the Monday email fires at 12:00 by construction. **Moving ICA's window earlier is therefore a real, evidence-backed option — and it is NOT taken here**, because the evidence has limits worth stating before anyone acts on it: exactly ONE of four products moved (n=1 week), the move observed was a campaign ENDING (price UP), which does not prove a new campaign APPEARS at the same moment, and one 03:00 run came back 404 on a product that answered fine at 06:00 — night traffic may meet a different wall than daytime traffic does. Also note the probe itself is the honest way to settle it: re-run it over more products and more weeks rather than reasoning from this paragraph — `probe.py` resolves `src` from its own location since the move, so it runs from a fresh clone without editing. **What is recorded here is the measurement, not a schedule change** — the schedule is edited by hand, per the read-only rule in the statistics bullet. Two things from the probe's own README that outlive it: (1) **its stated precondition is already met** — it said moving the window "kräver att schemafönstret görs Europe/Stockholm-medvetet först — det räknas i UTC idag", which v0.33.0 did, so the lever is now a value change rather than a rewrite; and (2) **Willys was already known to flip in 00:11–01:14 on Sunday night** (read off the tracker's own price history, which is why ICA was the open question at all) — a second store batching just after midnight, which is what made the midnight hypothesis worth testing.

## Ledger, fetcher, transport and the WAF

- **The politeness ledger is SHARED and lives in `infra/rate_limiter.py` (v0.14.0):** `StoreRateLimiter` (process-wide singleton via `providers.get_rate_limiter`, injected into the scheduler) is THE per-store request throttle — reserve-a-slot, keyed by store id. The scheduler spaces its background checks 60 s apart (`RATE_LIMIT_DELAY`); the **interactive** fetches (quick-add preview, first check, manual re-check in `admin.py`) now go through the SAME ledger with a short interval and a `max_wait` cap (env `QUICKADD_RATE_LIMIT_DELAY`=5 s, `QUICKADD_MAX_WAIT`=10 s) so they can't burst a store's WAF while never stalling a human on a background reservation. **Both cadences carry jitter on top of the floor (v0.24.0)** — `RATE_LIMIT_JITTER`=30 s background, env `QUICKADD_RATE_LIMIT_JITTER`=3 s interactive — so a store isn't hit on a clockwork beat; jitter only extends the *next* slot, never the current caller's wait (`max_wait` still holds). Before the shared ledger, interactive fetches bypassed all throttling — that is what let quick-add + scheduler checks trip ICA into HTTP 202/403. **Do not reintroduce the scheduler's old private `_last_request_at` dict.**

- **The fetcher fails honestly on a bot wall (v0.14.0; hardened v0.25.0):** `WebFetcher.fetch` never treats a wall as a page (the old `raise_for_status()` let a 202 through as an empty page → "metadata extraction failed" instead of "blocked"). **WAF statuses (HTTP 202/403/405/429) FAIL FAST — no retry — and return `blocked=True`:** ICA's 202 is an AWS WAF JS challenge (`awsWafCookieDomainList`/`gokuProps`, needs a real browser to mint an `aws-waf-token`) that a few-second retry cannot solve and only reinforces the IP flag. Only *transient* failures (5xx, network error, empty body) get the short bounded retry (`_RETRY_DELAYS_SECONDS`); a hard 4xx (404/410) fails fast. **405 is a wall, not a method error (v0.29.3, from prod evidence).** ICA answers 405 to a plain GET while it is walling us: we only ever GET, these are pages a browser opens, and the SAME urls returned 200, 405 AND 202 within one morning (Ketchup priced 06:42 → 405 at 10:01; Tortilla priced 09:42 → 405 at 10:03; Kaviar 405 at 07:56 → 202 at 10:13). The 405s arrive in RUNS starting on the first probe after a cooldown lapses. Classified as a hard 4xx it left `blocked=False`, so the breaker never re-tripped and the scheduler fed ICA at its 60-90 s cadence straight through the wall — and each 405 pushed its link +24h out of the förmiddag window. A genuine 404/410 is still a hard error and must NOT cool the store down. The `blocked` flag propagates `fetcher → perform_price_check` (`PriceCheckOutcome.blocked`) `→` **every caller** — scheduler AND the interactive endpoints — into the shared registry (→ store-block bullet). **The fetcher logs at the point of detection (v0.28.0):** a bot wall, a hard 4xx and a give-up-after-retries each get their own WARNING, because a WAF challenge used to produce *no log line at all* and the first evidence was a caller's generic "fetch failed". Fingerprint: consistent Chrome UA + `sec-ch-ua` + `sec-fetch-*` (bump `chrome_major` when stale) **plus HTTP/2 (v0.28.0, `httpx[http2]` → `h2`)** — a client claiming to be Chrome and then speaking HTTP/1.1 to a CDN contradicts itself at the connection layer, before a header is read; verified h2 negotiates against ica.se, willys.se and apotea.se — and **the negotiated protocol is logged once per host per process (v0.28.3)**, INFO for `HTTP/2` and WARNING for a 1.1 downgrade, so prod logs can prove which protocol each store actually got. None of this beats the JS challenge or the TLS/JA3 layer — `curl_cffi` shipped in v0.30.0 for the TLS/h2 half (→ transport bullet); a headless browser remains the un-built lever for the JS challenge itself. **But measure before building either (2026-07-26).** A probe run against the exact URL that had just been walled, 17 minutes after the 202: **plain httpx with these very headers got the full 510 KB product page**, from the same public IP that had just been refused. And the requests around the block were ~2 minutes apart — *slower* than the scheduler's own cadence. So that challenge was neither rate-driven nor fingerprint-driven; it was **sampled**. `curl_cffi` was installed and tried in the same probe (it also got 200) but proved nothing, because the control had already succeeded. **That probe also produced a wrong claim worth not repeating: its `http_version: 3` was read as HTTP/3. In curl's enum 3 is HTTP/2; HTTP/3 is 30.** curl_cffi buys TLS/JA3, h2 SETTINGS and header order — not a protocol upgrade (`_normalise_http_version` in the fetcher now encodes that, with a test). **It was ported anyway in v0.30.0** — not on the strength of the theory, but because the change is small, reversible and finally MEASURABLE (→ transport bullet). A headless browser that mints an `aws-waf-token` and hands it to the cheap fetcher is the only lever that addresses the JS challenge itself; curl_cffi cannot solve one. **That lever was measured, it works, and it was declined anyway — read the waf-token bullet before anyone proposes it again.**

- **A browser-minted `aws-waf-token` gets curl_cffi through ICA's wall — measured 2026-07-29, DECIDED AGAINST 2026-08-13.** The spike CLAUDE.md kept demanding before a headless browser was built has been run, from the dev box (`80.216.252.59` — the SAME public IP as the Dokploy VM, verified, so the flag being tested is prod's own). Provoke a wall, mint a token in Chromium, then alternate bare and token-bearing curl_cffi requests **seconds apart** so the wall is provably still up between them — the interleaving is the whole design, because the 2026-07-26 probe concluded nothing precisely by testing after the wall had lifted. Result, three clean rounds: **bare 202 (2492 B) 3/3, `aws-waf-token` 200 (512 711 B) 3/3.** Four facts a sidecar design must be built on, each measured rather than assumed: (1) **the token is HOST-wide, not per-URL** — one mint on the toalettpapper page opened three other products including one in a *different* ICA butik (store id 1003396 vs 1004503), so one browser run covers all 44 ICA links; (2) **a token is only minted when the challenge is SERVED** — run the browser against an unflagged IP and it gets the page directly with no token to hand over, which means mint-on-demand (trigger on the 202) is the only workable trigger, and pre-warming is not a thing; (3) **the browser MUST override its user agent** — Playwright's default carries `HeadlessChrome`, and ICA answers that with a hard CloudFront 403 and NO challenge, so no token; with an ordinary Chrome UA the challenge is served, solved in JS, and the real product page renders (title check, ~776 KB); (4) **judge the browser on CONTENT, not on the navigation status** — the challenge itself answers 202 and the real page arrives after the reload, so a spike that returns early on `status != 200` reports failure on a success (this one did, on its first pass). Also observed: the wall lifts on its own in **~4–5 minutes**, and the token was still opening the page 9 minutes after minting. **The judgement that follows from all this: the scheduler does not need a token, it needs patience — and it already has it** (breaker + `next_check_at` deferral, and ICA links are weekly anyway). What is actually harmed by a wall is the HUMAN path — a quick-add refused with 503 because the breaker is open, which is what prompted the spike. **And that is the harm that stopped happening, which is why no sidecar was ever built (decided with Magnus 2026-08-13).** `check_attempts` over its whole prod life at that point — 2026-07-27 to 2026-08-13, 395 checks — holds **5 blocked rows, all ICA**: four on 2026-07-29 (the spike's own day, under the burst pattern v0.39.0's stratification then removed) and one on 2026-08-12, whose very next check on the same product succeeded **five minutes later** — the breaker's base cooldown doing exactly its job. **Exactly ONE of the five was on the human path**, the 2026-07-29 quick-add preview that prompted the spike; there has not been another since. A sidecar is a Playwright dependency, a browser in the image and a token cache, standing ready for an event that fires once a fortnight and self-heals in five minutes. **Do not build it on the strength of the measurement above — the measurement says the token works, not that it is needed.** If ICA's walls come back (watch `check_attempts.outcome = 'blocked'`, not the logs), the four facts here are what a design starts from — **and the harness that produced them is still in the repo**: `docs/spikes/spike_handover.py` (does a browser-minted token survive the handover to curl_cffi?) and `docs/spikes/spike_scope.py` (how BROAD and how LONG-LIVED is one token?). They are standalone scripts, not tests — nothing imports them, CI never runs them (`testpaths = ["tests"]`) and Playwright is deliberately not a dependency: each imports it lazily inside the browser step and points `CHROME` at a hardcoded path in the dev box's `~/.cache/ms-playwright/`, so on any other machine that line is the first thing to fix, not evidence that the spike rotted. What they cannot be run without is a genuinely flagged IP — the wall has to be up for the interleaving to prove anything, which is exactly why these numbers were expensive to get and should be re-measured rather than re-derived.

- **The fetch transport is swappable and instrumented (v0.30.0):** `WebFetcher` builds a **curl_cffi** `AsyncSession` (`impersonate="chrome"`) by default and an httpx client when env **`FETCHER_TRANSPORT=httpx`** says so; `fetcher.transport` records which one got built and every protocol **and bot-wall** log line names it (the wall line since v0.30.1 — the transport is the ONE thing `check_attempts` does not record, so during a control week the log is what attributes a wall to a transport; the durable COUNT is the table, see below). curl_cffi closes the half of the fingerprint headers cannot reach — the **TLS ClientHello (JA3)** and the **HTTP/2 SETTINGS/priority frames** — because a client sending Chrome's headers over an OpenSSL-default handshake contradicts itself before a byte of HTTP is read. **It does NOT solve the JS challenge** (no JS engine, so no `aws-waf-token` ever), and its benefit against ICA is **PROVEN as of 2026-08-13, on 18 days of prod evidence.** First datapoint (2026-07-28): the 14 ICA links walled under httpx on Monday morning (405/202 runs) ALL passed under curl_cffi within 24h — same URLs, same IP, same förmiddag window. One clean day was a datapoint and not a verdict (the 2026-07-26 probe showed ICA's challenge behaving *sampled*), so the verdict waited for the run: **2026-07-27 → 2026-08-13, 395 checks, 382 ok and 5 blocked — all five ICA, four of them on one day, and the fifth self-cleared in five minutes.** ICA campaigns arrive intact and no wall has cost a price point since. **Measure this in `check_attempts`, NOT with `docker logs | grep 'Bot wall'`** — the recipe this bullet used to give: the container's log starts empty at every deploy, so on any day prod was redeployed the grep answers "0 walls" for the whole period and it reads as proof (hit while verifying this very paragraph, against a container 20 minutes old). `outcome='blocked'` is the durable record and it is what `check_attempts` was built for. The env var stays the rollback: one restart flips to httpx. Under curl_cffi the UA/`sec-ch-ua` come from the impersonation profile: **never hand-write them on top**, that re-creates the contradiction (`_CHROME_MAJOR` feeds the httpx path only). A missing or broken native lib degrades to httpx with a WARNING rather than leaving the app with no fetcher at all. `curl-cffi` ships prebuilt abi3 wheels, so the image needs no toolchain; **httpx stays regardless** — OpenRouter and Resend use it, and it is the fallback.

- **The scheduler self-defends against store WAFs (v0.23.0 pause, v0.25.0 breaker):** (1) **Auto-pause** — the add flows (`/quick-add/preview`, `/quick-add`, `POST /products/{id}/stores`) call `scheduler.pause_for(...)`, silencing background checks for a window each add pushes forward (env `SCHEDULER_ADD_PAUSE_MINUTES`=60) so a burst of manual adds can't race the scheduler into a store's WAF; `paused_until` shows in `/scheduler/status` and the portal status box (PAUSAD — parse it with `parseUtc`, it's naive UTC). (2) **Per-store circuit breaker** — see the store-block bullet below; the scheduler is now one of its clients, not its owner. **A failed weekday-scheduled check retries in the NEXT day's förmiddag (v0.39.0 — `schedule.next_morning_retry`, never raw `now+24h` arithmetic in the scheduler: +24h kept the clock time the failure happened at, so a link that 404:ed at 12:01 local retried at 12:01 forever, outside the window and drifting across weekdays; note a permanently dead URL retries every morning until a human deletes it — **and that is the answer, not an open question (closed 2026-08-13)**: `domain/link_health.py` flags it after three straight non-blocked failures, Fel & luckor lists it with the store's own words and a "sidan är borta" hint, and the link is deleted from its edit dialog. Measured on the one case prod has produced: an ICA link 404:ed on five consecutive mornings (2026-07-29 → 08-02) and was then deleted by hand — five wasted requests, spread across five days, on a store that answers 44 other links fine. **Auto-retirement is DECIDED AGAINST at this scale**: it would need a threshold, and every threshold deletes a link during a store outage or a URL scheme change, which is unrecoverable (the URL is the link's identity and its history hangs off it), to save a handful of requests a human clears in a week. `is_active` is not the escape hatch either — prod has 100 links and 0 inactive ones; nothing has ever been retired rather than deleted). A BLOCKED one is not a failed one (v0.29.2):** a wall is the store's state, not the link's failure, so the discovering link is deferred to the breaker's `blocked_until` — the same deferral the skip branch already gave every *other* due link of that store. **Since v0.39.0 those deferrals are STAGGERED, not stamped with one timestamp**: the discoverer takes `blocked_until` itself (the probe when the cooldown lapses) and each further deferral of that store lands at least `RATE_LIMIT_DELAY` plus jitter behind the previous one (per-cycle counter, so repeated cycles against a still-cooled store re-spread from zero instead of marching outward). Identical timestamps meant the whole store came due at once and drained on the ledger's 60 s floor back to back — the exact burst that preceded both ICA walls measured 2026-07-29, a self-reinforcing loop (block → identical timestamps → burst → block), and it left 3 links on the same microsecond in prod. Before that, whichever link happened to sit first in the ASC due queue took a 24h penalty for being first while its siblings resumed in minutes, and since +24h keeps the *clock time the wall happened at*, a link blocked at 23:16 retried at 23:16 the next night, and the night after — permanently outside the 06–12 window `domain/schedule.py` exists to hold it in. That was the only mechanism moving checks out of that window. **A blocked link also no longer sets `last_checked_at`** (we never saw the page), so "Senast kollad" and the portal's freshness line stop reporting a wall as fresh data; a fetch that *reached* the store and yielded no price still counts, because the store answered.

- **The circuit breaker is SHARED and lives in `infra/store_block.py` (v0.28.0):** `StoreBlockRegistry` (process-wide singleton via `providers.get_block_registry`, injected into the scheduler) is THE record of "this store is walling us right now". It answers *whether* we may talk to a store; the ledger answers *how fast*. **Every outgoing store call consults it and reports back** — the scheduler skips a cooled store's due links without fetching (deferring `next_check_at` past the cooldown), and the interactive endpoints (`/quick-add/preview`, `/check/{id}`) refuse with **HTTP 503 + `Retry-After`** and a Swedish "butiken blockerar oss" message. It was a private dict inside `PriceCheckScheduler` until v0.28.0, which meant only *background* checks stood down: a human seeing "kunde inte hämta sidan" could mash "Kolla nu" straight into an active AWS WAF challenge, which is precisely what keeps an IP flag alive. Conversely a block found interactively now silences the scheduler. **The cooldown escalates** — base × 2ⁿ, env `SCHEDULER_STORE_BLOCK_COOLDOWN_MINUTES`=**5 since v0.29.1** (was 30; name kept for compatibility) capped by `STORE_BLOCK_MAX_COOLDOWN_MINUTES`=240 — and **the first page that loads resets the strike count**: the predicate is "reached the store" (`failure_reason != "fetch_failed"` — a fetch that succeeded but yielded no price still counts as "the store answered"), and since v0.28.2 the scheduler and the interactive paths use that SAME predicate (the scheduler briefly keyed on full success, so a store whose page layout broke kept stale strikes and the next real block escalated from the wrong level). A lapsed cooldown clears but the strike count survives, so the probe after a lapse escalates rather than restarting at base. In-memory/single-process like the add-pause; the scheduler's DB-side `next_check_at` deferral survives a restart anyway. `blocked_stores` is in `/scheduler/status` and the portal status box shows **BLOCKERAD** (outranking PAUSAD).

- **Every outgoing store request goes through the ledger — including the Willys REST API (v0.28.0).** `WillysApiExtractor._fetch_product` fired straight out of `httpx` with no slot and DEBUG-only logging, so a Willys price check made **two** requests to `www.willys.se` (the page fetch in `perform_price_check`, then the API call from the extraction ladder) while spending **one** slot, and a bot wall there was indistinguishable from a missing product. It now reserves a slot keyed `"host:www.willys.se"` (the extractor never sees a store id, and the host is what a WAF rate-limits) and logs 202/403/429 as a bot wall at WARNING. **Since v0.28.2 the API call also rides the SHARED `WebFetcher` client** (`fetch_json` — same Chrome fingerprint, same h2 connection, per-request headers repainted as an XHR) instead of a per-call bare `httpx` client that announced `python-httpx` over HTTP/1.1 to the very host the page fetch had just spoken Chrome-h2 to. **Since v0.30.1 the repaint also REMOVES the navigation-only headers** (`sec-fetch-user`, `upgrade-insecure-requests` — a real Chrome XHR never sends them, but both transports paint them onto every request) **and claims the product page as `Referer`** (`sec-fetch-site: same-origin` with no Referer contradicts itself). Removal is transport-specific and non-obvious: an empty string value does NOT remove a header on either transport — both SEND it empty, echo-verified — so curl_cffi gets `None` (its "disable this header" marker) and httpx gets the header deleted from a built request. **A wall in the API raises `domain.result.StoreBlockedError`** rather than returning None (None means "product missing" and fell through to a pointless LLM run that ended as a strike-RESETTING `no_price`): the parser lets it through the ladder's `except Exception`, `perform_price_check` turns it into a `blocked=True` outcome, and the quick-add preview's API tier trips the breaker + answers 503 instead of firing the page fetch at the same walled host. The only outgoing calls that are deliberately *not* store-gated are OpenRouter (`domain/parser.py`) and Resend (`infra/email.py`) — neither is a tracked store.

## check_attempts

**`check_attempts` is what the stores REFUSED to say (v0.35.0).** `price_points` only ever recorded SUCCESS: a failed check writes nothing, and since v0.29.2 a blocked one does not even touch `last_checked_at` (a wall is not an observation). So "how often does ICA actually answer us" was unanswerable from stored data — the only trace was a WARNING in the in-memory ring buffer, which resets on restart. It shipped **before** the statistics page it exists to feed, because a page can be built whenever and history cannot be backfilled.

- **One row per CHECK, not per HTTP request** (a Willys check makes two requests; what is measured is "did we get a price"), and **a skip is not an attempt** — when the breaker is open the scheduler defers a store's due links without fetching, and a row per deferral would measure the schedule instead of the stores.

- **`CheckAttemptLog` writes in its OWN transaction** (`infra/check_log.py`), and that is the design, not a detail: the interactive endpoints raise their HTTPException on a failed or blocked check *before* they commit, so a row added to the request's session is rolled back — losing precisely the attempts worth having. It also **never raises**: telemetry that can kill a price check trades the data for the thing the data is about.

- **The write lives inside `perform_price_check`**, wrapping the flow so every exit — ok / no_price / fetch_failed / blocked, plus an escaped exception as `error` — is recorded from one place. `attempt_log` defaults to None (recording OFF) because several hundred tests drive the flow with mock sessions, so the default cannot be the enforcement: **an AST gate in `test_static_gates.py` fails the build if any `perform_price_check(...)` call in `src/` omits `attempt_log=`.** The quick-add PREVIEW is the one caller that never reaches the flow (it fetches before a link exists), so it records via `_record_store_outcome(..., log_attempt=True)` — `product_store_id` is nullable for exactly that, and `ON DELETE SET NULL` so removing a link cannot fail on an audit row.

- **`domain.result.extraction_source()` is THE reader of `raw_response["source"]`** — the same `isinstance(raw, dict)` dance had been written three times over a loose JSONB dict where a typo returns None and reads as "the LLM answered". It returns the tier verbatim (`willys_api` / `<store>_page` / `jsonld` / `product_gone` / `none` / `unknown`; rows before v0.66.0 may carry `llm:<model>`, the removed model tier). `checks_llm` survives on `/scheduler/status` because a wire field is append-only — nothing increments it on purpose any more, so a non-zero value means a success from a tier the scheduler does not recognise. The scheduler gained a `checks_page_state` counter: Rusta and Clas Ohlson checks used to fall through to the else and inflate `checks_llm`, the one number anyone reads to judge what the cascade costs, while those tiers call no model at all.

- **Statistics must NEVER feed back into fetch cadence** (decided with Magnus, 2026-07-28). Not automatically, and not via an "apply" button. Raising frequency where prices move most means fetching hardest exactly where the WAF lives, and a wall records no price point — so the product that changes price most often would come to look like it had stopped changing. The loop would eat the measurement it is built on. The planned statistics page is therefore READ-ONLY by construction: no write endpoints at all, weekday patterns shown as observations, and any schedule change made by hand in the link dialog.

## Scheduler leadership

**Scheduler leadership: one per database, and none where the installation says so (v0.64.0).** The scheduler runs inside the web process, so every copy of the app used to run one. Two different ways that goes wrong, and each has its own tool, because neither covers the other. **Two instances on ONE database** (a second replica, or a rollout's overlap) are kept apart by `infra/leader_lock.py`: a Postgres session-level advisory lock held on a dedicated AUTOCOMMIT connection and asked every cycle. The holder checks and mails; the others serve the portal and log `STANDBY` once. If the holder's connection dies, Postgres frees the lock and a standby takes over on its next cycle. **A lock that cannot be asked means standby**, since working unguarded is the double-checking the lock exists to prevent. **Two installations with SEPARATE databases** (a lab copy of production, a cutover window) cannot see each other's lock, so `SCHEDULER_ENABLED=false` switches one off. It is parsed strictly: unset or empty means on, a true/false word is obeyed, and anything else raises at startup, because a typo read as "off" silently stops production's mail and one read as "on" silently defeats the switch. The state is never implicit: `/scheduler/status` and `/health` carry `enabled` and `role` (`disabled` / `leader` / `standby`), and the sidebar says "Schemaläggare avstängd" or "vilar". **A lost lock connection is INVALIDATED, not just closed**: it comes from the shared request pool, and a plain close() handed the dead connection back to it, where the next checkout failed. The two-instance test (`tests/test_scheduler_leadership.py`, real Postgres, `pg_terminate_backend` on the holder) failed 4 runs in 6 before that fix and 10 in 10 passed after. Manual checks ("Kolla nu", quick-add) are human-initiated and run regardless of role.
